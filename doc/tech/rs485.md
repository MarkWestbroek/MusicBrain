# RS‑485

## What it is
An **electrical standard** (TIA/EIA‑485) for differential serial signalling on a twisted pair. Like CAN it is multi‑drop and noise‑tolerant; unlike CAN it defines *only the physical layer*, not framing, addressing, arbitration or error handling — you bring your own protocol on top.

Typical numbers: up to 32 standard nodes (more with low‑load transceivers), 1.2 km at 100 kbit/s, ~10 Mbit/s over short runs. Half‑duplex (one pair) is most common; full‑duplex (two pairs) exists.

## Why we use it (ADR 0006)
- **Fallback** for the inter‑case bus when CAN‑FD silicon is inconvenient.
- Easier to drive from any UART‑equipped MCU plus a cheap transceiver (MAX485, SN75176, SP3485).

## What matters for MusicBrain
- Use a **MAX13487E** or **SP3485** auto‑direction transceiver if you don't want to handle DE/RE pin toggling manually.
- Frame format on top of RS‑485 would be the same [SPI frame](../protocols/spi-frame.md) with the same CRC — bridge nodes just speak it over UART instead of CAN.
- Terminate the line with 120 Ω at each end, bias resistors on the master end to keep the bus idle high.

## Gotchas vs. CAN‑FD
- **No arbitration** — collisions are your problem. Use a master‑polled protocol.
- **No automatic CRC / retransmit** — our frame‑level CRC is what catches errors; the application retries.
- **No standard connectors** — pick one and document it (XLR3 / RJ45 are common but please don't share connectors with audio or Ethernet on the same chassis).
- Slightly more error‑prone in heavy electrical noise than CAN, because there's no per‑bit error signalling.
